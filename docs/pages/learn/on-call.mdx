# On-Call

On-Call turns an alert into a person's phone ringing. An alert on its own posts to a channel and hopes somebody is watching. A page goes to whoever is on call and keeps escalating to the next person until a human acknowledges it.

Setting it up takes five steps. The first four are done once by whoever runs the rotation, and the fifth by every responder for themselves:

| | What you do | Who | How often |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | [Create a team](#step-1-create-a-team) of responders | An organization owner or admin | Once per team |
| **2** | [Build a schedule](#step-2-build-a-schedule) that says who is on call when | An organization owner or admin | Once per rotation |
| **3** | [Create an escalation policy](#step-3-create-an-escalation-policy) that says who is paged first and who is next | An organization owner or admin | Once per policy |
| **4** | [Connect your alerts](#step-4-connect-your-alerts-to-the-policy) to the policy | Anyone with write access to the project | Once per project |
| **5** | [Add your contact methods](#step-5-add-your-contact-methods) | Every responder | Once per person |

Then [send a test page](#check-that-it-works) to see the whole chain work. Steps 1 to 3 happen on the **On-Call** page in the sidebar. You can also do every step [from the CLI or through an AI agent](#configure-on-call-with-the-cli-or-mcp).

## Step 1: Create a team

A team is the group of people who answer for a set of projects. Schedules belong to a team, so it comes first.

1. Open **On-Call** in the sidebar and select the **Teams** tab.
2. Click **New Team**.
3. Enter a **Name** and a **Description**, tick the **Members**, and under **Owned projects** tick the projects this team answers for.
4. Click **New Team**.

<img src="/on-call/oncall-teams.png" alt="The On-Call page in the sidebar with the Teams tab selected. Two teams are listed with their members, owned projects and number of schedules, and a New Team button sits on the right" style={{ maxWidth: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

<img src="/on-call/oncall-team-dialog.png" alt="The team dialog with a name, a description, four ticked members, their order, and the owned project checkout-api" style={{ maxWidth: '420px', width: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

A project has at most one owning team. The issue detail page uses that link to show who is on call for the project you are looking at.

**Member order** is only how the team is listed. It is not a rotation: an escalation step that targets the team pages every member at once, and each schedule layer keeps its own order.

## Step 2: Build a schedule

A schedule answers one question: who is on call right now. It belongs to a team, has its own timezone, and is built from **layers**. Each layer is a rotation over a list of people.

1. Select the **Schedules** tab and click **New Schedule**.
2. Enter a **Name**, pick the **Team**, and pick the **Timezone** the responders live in. Handoff times are read in this timezone. Leave it empty to use the organization's timezone.
3. Click **New Schedule**. The new schedule opens below the list, still without layers.

<img src="/on-call/new-schedule-dialog.png" alt="The New Schedule dialog with the name Platform secondary, a description, the team Platform and the timezone Europe/Berlin" style={{ maxWidth: '420px', width: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

4. Click **Edit Layers**, then **Add Layer**, and fill in the layer:

| Field | What to enter |
|-------|---------------|
| **Rotation** | **Daily**, **Weekly**, or **Custom interval** (every N days) |
| **Handoff time** | The time of day the shift changes hands |
| **Handoff day** | The weekday of the handoff, for weekly rotations |
| **Rotation start** | The date the rotation counts from |
| **Members (rotation order)** | The people to rotate through, in order. Use **Add member...** to pick anyone in the organization. |
| **Restrictions** | Optional. Limits the layer to certain hours (**Daily**, for example 18:00 to 09:00) or certain days (**Weekly**, for example Saturday 00:00 to Monday 09:00). Without one the layer covers every minute. |

5. Add more layers if different people cover different hours. A layer further down the list wins where two overlap.
6. Click **Update Schedule**.

<img src="/on-call/oncall-layer-editor.png" alt="The Schedules tab with the schedule Payments Primary open. The layer editor shows a Business hours layer restricted to Monday 09:00 through Friday 18:00 and a Nights and weekends layer below it, and the timeline underneath shows one row per layer and a combined Schedule row" style={{ maxWidth: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

The timeline under the editor shows the result. There is one row per layer, and the bottom **Schedule** row is who actually gets paged. Read it for gaps before you move on: where no layer covers a moment, nobody is on call, and an escalation step that targets the schedule reaches nobody.

[How a schedule picks the person on call](#how-a-schedule-picks-the-person-on-call) explains stacking, restrictions and daylight saving in detail.

## Step 3: Create an escalation policy

A policy is the ordered list of who to wake, and how long to wait before moving on to the next.

1. Select the **Policies** tab and click **New Policy**.
2. Enter a **Name**.
3. In **Level 1**, pick the kind of target in the first dropdown and the target itself in the second. For the first level, choose **Schedule** and the schedule from step 2. A level can have several targets.
4. Set **Escalate after** to the number of minutes the page waits for an acknowledgement before it moves to the next level.
5. Click **Add Step** for each further level, for example a second schedule and then the whole team.
6. Set **Repeat** and **Urgency** if you need them (see below), check the **Preview**, and click **New Policy**.

<img src="/on-call/oncall-policy-dialog.png" alt="The policy dialog with three levels: the schedule Payments Primary with 5 minutes, the schedule Payments Secondary with 10 minutes, and the team Payments with 15 minutes. Repeat is 1, Urgency is Auto, and the Preview shows the chain" style={{ maxWidth: '560px', width: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

A target is one of:

| Target | Who is notified |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Schedule** | Whoever is on call for that schedule at the moment the step runs |
| **Team** | Every member of the team |
| **User** | That specific person |
| **Channel** | A notification channel, to post the page into Slack or a webhook alongside the people being paged |

**Repeat** sends the whole chain around again after the last step, up to five more times. Use it when a page must not go unanswered overnight. With repeat at zero the page stays open after the last step, but nothing further is sent.

**Urgency** decides which of each responder's two [notification rule](#notification-rules) chains runs:

| Setting | Behaviour |
|---------|-----------|
| **Auto** (default) | Critical severity becomes high urgency, everything else becomes low |
| **Always high** | Every page from this policy is high urgency |
| **Always low** | Every page from this policy is low urgency |

## Step 4: Connect your alerts to the policy

Paging is wired in through [Alerts](/learn/alerts). A channel of the type **Escalation policy** opens a page where other channels send a message.

1. Open **Alerts** in the sidebar, select the **Channels** tab and click **New Channel**.
2. Enter a **Name**, set **Type** to **Escalation policy**, and select your policy under **Escalation Policy**. The list holds the policies of the organization that owns the project.
3. Click **New Channel**.
4. On the **Rules** tab, create a rule or edit one, and select this channel as its **Channel**.

<img src="/on-call/oncall-channel-dialog.png" alt="The New Channel dialog with Type set to Escalation policy, an Escalation Policy dropdown, and a note that testing an escalation channel opens a real page" style={{ maxWidth: '420px', width: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

From now on, when one of those rules fires, a page opens and the policy starts at level 1.

## Step 5: Add your contact methods

Contact methods are how Traceway reaches you when you are paged. They are personal: every responder adds their own, and nobody else can read or change them.

This is the step teams forget. Paging still works without it, because a responder with no contact methods is paged on their account email, but nobody wants to find that out during an incident.

1. Open **Account** in the sidebar and select the **Contact Methods** tab.
2. Click **New Contact Method**, pick the **Type** and fill in its fields:

| Type | What you enter |
|------|----------------|
| **Email** | Optional address. Leave it blank to use your account email. |
| **Slack** | Incoming webhook URL, with optional channel and username |
| **Pushover** | User key and app token |
| **Telegram** | Bot token and chat ID |
| **SMS (Twilio)** | Phone number in international format, for example `+12025550123`. Only offered when the server [has Twilio configured](#sms-and-twilio). |

3. Click **New Contact Method**.
4. Click the paper plane icon in the method's row to send yourself a test message.

<img src="/on-call/oncall-contact-method-dialog.png" alt="The New Contact Method dialog with Type set to Email and an optional email field that defaults to the account email" style={{ maxWidth: '420px', width: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

<img src="/on-call/contact-methods-tab.png" alt="The Contact Methods tab of the Account page listing a Slack method and an Email method, each with an Enabled switch and icons to test, edit and delete" style={{ maxWidth: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

The **Enabled** switch silences a method without deleting it.

A phone number has to be verified before it is paged. Adding one sends a six digit code, which expires after ten minutes and allows five attempts. At most three codes are sent to a number per hour.

### Notification rules

By default a page notifies all your enabled contact methods at once. Notification rules replace that with an ordered chain, one for high urgency pages and one for low.

1. On the **Account** page, select the **Notification Rules** tab.
2. Under **High urgency**, click **Add step**, choose a contact method and the number of minutes after which it is tried. Add a step for each further method.
3. Do the same under **Low urgency**, then click **Update Notification Rules**.

<img src="/on-call/notification-rules-tab.png" alt="The Notification Rules tab of the Account page. High urgency notifies Slack after 0 minutes and the account email after 5 minutes, low urgency notifies Slack only" style={{ maxWidth: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

A typical high urgency chain nudges you quietly first and then gets louder: Slack at once, a push notification after 2 minutes, SMS after 5. Delays count from the moment the page reaches you. Acknowledging cancels every step that has not fired yet, so taking the incident in the first thirty seconds means your phone never rings.

Low urgency usually deserves a shorter chain, often a single Slack message. A chain you leave empty falls back to notifying every enabled method at once. It does not silence pages.

## Check that it works

Send a test page through the channel from step 4. It is a real page and runs the real escalation, so it is the honest way to find out whether your rotation and your policy work.

1. Open **Alerts**, select the **Channels** tab and click the paper plane icon in the row of your escalation channel.
2. Click **Send Test**.

<img src="/on-call/test-channel-dialog.png" alt="The Test Channel dialog: testing Platform pager opens a real page and notifies whoever is on call, exactly as a firing rule would, with Cancel and Send Test buttons" style={{ maxWidth: '420px', width: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

3. Open **On-Call**. The **Pages** tab shows `Test page from channel "<name>"` as **Open**, and the person on call for level 1 receives it through their contact methods.
4. Acknowledge it from the notification or with the single check mark in the row, then resolve it with the double check mark.

<img src="/on-call/test-page-open.png" alt="The Pages tab of the On-Call page with one row: Test page from channel Platform pager, severity Info, low urgency, level 1 of 2, status Open, and the acknowledge and resolve icons on the right" style={{ maxWidth: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

Two things to know about the test:

- **It is low urgency.** The test page has `info` severity, so a policy left on **Auto** runs each responder's low urgency chain.
- **Pressing Test twice does not open two pages.** While the previous test page of the same channel is unresolved, another test only raises its event count. Resolve the test page when you are done.

To check the rotation without paging anyone, read the timeline of the schedule or the [Overview tab](#see-who-is-on-call).

## Respond to a page

The **Pages** tab is the queue of the current project, and the badge on **On-Call** in the sidebar counts its open pages. For the pages of every project at once, use the organization's [On-Call page](/learn/organization-overview#on-call).

<img src="/on-call/oncall-pages.png" alt="The On-Call page with the Pages tab selected and two pages in the queue, one open and one acknowledged. The On-Call item in the sidebar carries a badge with the number of open pages" style={{ maxWidth: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Open** | Nobody has taken it. Escalation is running. |
| **Acknowledged** | Somebody is on it. Escalation has stopped, and queued deliveries are cancelled. |
| **Resolved** | Finished. The same condition can open a fresh page. |

**Acknowledge** stops the escalation immediately and cancels anything still queued for delivery, including the later steps of your own notification chain. **Resolve** closes the page. Both are in the row and in the page's detail view.

Neither needs write access to the project. A responder with a read-only role who is paged at 3am can still take the incident and close it out.

<img src="/on-call/oncall-page-detail.png" alt="The detail view of an acknowledged page: the rule, the event count, the escalation chain with level 1 highlighted, and a delivery timeline showing an email that was sent and who acknowledged" style={{ maxWidth: '480px', width: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

Click a row to open the detail view. It shows the escalation chain with the current level highlighted, and every delivery attempt with its outcome. That log is the thing to read when somebody says they never got paged. Personal destinations are masked in it: a phone number shows its last four digits, an email override its first character and domain. Account emails are shown in full, since everyone in the organization can already see them.

### Acknowledge without logging in

Every notification addressed to a person carries its own acknowledge link. It opens a summary of the page with an acknowledge button and needs no session, so it works from a phone with no password manager in reach.

<img src="/on-call/oncall-ack-link.png" alt="The acknowledge page opened from a link: the page's subject, severity, project, rule and event count, and a line saying who acknowledged it and when" style={{ maxWidth: '520px', width: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

The link can acknowledge that one page and nothing else. It gives no dashboard access, cannot resolve, and stops working once the page is resolved. Opening it only shows the page, so an email scanner that follows links cannot acknowledge your incident by accident. The acknowledgement is attributed to the person the notification was addressed to, and the page records that it came in through a link.

Deliveries to a **Channel** target are the exception, since a shared Slack room has no single owner. Those carry the ordinary dashboard link.

## Cover a shift with an override

An override puts someone else on call for a stretch of time without touching the rotation. Use it for a doctor's appointment, a swapped weekend, or a vacation. Any member of the organization can add one, because covering for a teammate should not need an administrator.

1. On the **Schedules** tab, click the schedule.
2. Click **Add Override**.
3. Pick the **User** who covers, the **Start** and the **End**. The times are in your local timezone. An override can cover at most 30 days.
4. Click **New Override**.

<img src="/on-call/oncall-override-dialog.png" alt="The Add Override dialog with a user, a start and an end" style={{ maxWidth: '420px', width: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

An override beats every layer, and where two overrides overlap the one created most recently wins. It shows on the timeline as a dashed block labelled `OVERRIDE` and ends on its own when its time is up.

<img src="/on-call/oncall-schedule-detail.png" alt="A schedule with its timeline and override list. The Schedule row contains a dashed block labelled OVERRIDE, and the override is listed below with a delete icon" style={{ maxWidth: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px', borderRadius: '8px', border: '1px solid #e5e7eb' }} />

An override can be deleted by whoever created it, by the person covering, or by an organization owner or admin.

## Common setups

### One engineer, one rotation

The smallest useful configuration. One team, one schedule with a single weekly layer over everybody, and one policy with a single step that targets the schedule and waits 15 minutes. Set **Repeat** to 1 or 2 so an unanswered page comes back around.

### Business hours and out of hours

Two layers on one schedule. The first covers the working week and rotates over the whole team. The second covers nights and weekends and rotates over the smaller group that signed up for it. Restrictions on both keep them out of each other's way, and the second wins anywhere they overlap. This is the configuration in the [layer editor screenshot](#step-2-build-a-schedule).

### Primary and secondary

Two schedules on the same team, and a policy with two steps. Step one targets the primary schedule and waits five minutes. Step two targets the secondary. A third step that targets the whole team makes a decent last resort.

### Loud for critical, quiet for the rest

Two policies. The critical one keeps **Urgency** on **Auto** and escalates quickly. The other sets it to **Always low** with one step and a long delay, and each responder points their low urgency chain at Slack only. Attach noisy rules to the second policy and they will never ring a phone.

### Vacation cover

Add an [override](#cover-a-shift-with-an-override) for the dates. It beats the rotation without editing it and disappears on its own when the dates pass. There is no need to reshuffle the layer and remember to put it back.

## How it works

An alert rule fires, and instead of sending a message it opens a **page**. The page walks an **escalation policy**. Each step of the policy names targets, and a target resolves to people through a **schedule**, a **team**, or a direct user. Each of those people is then reached through their own **contact methods**, in the order their **notification rules** define.

<img src="/on-call/how-the-pieces-fit-together.svg" alt="An alert rule opens a page, which walks an escalation policy level by level until somebody acknowledges" style={{ maxWidth: '100%', height: 'auto', marginTop: '16px' }} />

Two ideas are worth holding onto:

- **The page is the unit of work, not the message.** A noisy rule that fires two hundred times produces one page. Re-fires raise an event counter and never restart the escalation clock.
- **Escalation stops on acknowledge, not on delivery.** Sending a message proves nothing. Traceway keeps climbing the policy until somebody acknowledges, or until the policy is exhausted.

### One page per problem

While a page is unresolved, the same rule and dedup token raise the event count of the existing page instead of opening a new one. The escalation clock is untouched, and nobody is notified again. Rules without a dedup token deduplicate at the rule level, so one noisy rule is one page.

This is what keeps a flapping error from turning into a pager storm. A rule that fires sixty times over half an hour produces one page and exactly the notifications its policy calls for. Resolving the page releases the key, so the same condition can open a fresh page afterwards.

### How a schedule picks the person on call

Layers stack, and a layer later in the list takes precedence over the ones before it. At any instant at most one person is on call for the schedule: the member whose turn it is in the highest layer that covers that moment. Overrides sit above every layer. If nothing covers the moment, nobody is on call.

A restriction narrows a layer to certain hours or days:

- **Daily**, for example 18:00 to 09:00. A window that ends earlier than it starts wraps past midnight.
- **Weekly**, for example Saturday 00:00 to Monday 09:00. These can span several days.

The schedule's timezone is what handoff times and restrictions mean. Set it to where the people live, not where the servers live. A rotation set to `Europe/Berlin` hands off at 09:00 Berlin time all year, through daylight saving on both sides.

On a spring-forward day a time inside the skipped hour resolves back an hour, so a window ending in that hour is an hour shorter that night. A short window that reaches into the gap can collapse to nothing, in which case the layer covers nothing that day and only that day.

The timeline renders in the schedule's timezone and marks the current moment with a red line. The **Day**, **Week**, **2 Weeks** and **Month** controls change how much it shows.

### How a policy escalates

A page snapshots its policy the moment it opens, so editing a policy never changes pages that are already escalating. Inside that snapshot, targets are still resolved to people when the step runs. A schedule target reaches whoever is on call at that moment, including an override that started five minutes ago.

Within a single step, each person is notified once. Someone who appears on two targets of the same step does not get two pages.

Urgency is resolved once when the page opens and then remembered. A low severity re-fire can never quietly downgrade a page that is already escalating at high urgency.

A freshly opened page does not wait for a polling interval. The escalator is woken directly, so the first level is notified within a second or so.

### Delivery guarantees

Nothing is sent from inside the request that triggered it. A firing rule only opens the page, in its own transaction. The escalator then claims that page and, in a single transaction, advances the escalation level and writes every delivery for that level into a durable outbox. A separate worker drains the outbox and does the sending.

Because the level advance and the deliveries commit together, a crash mid-incident cannot lose a page or enqueue a level twice. Sending itself is at-least-once by design. A delivery interrupted between the send and the record of it is reclaimed about five minutes later and retried, so a crash at exactly the wrong moment can repeat one message. Paging twice is the right failure mode when the alternative is not paging at all.

Deliveries that fail are retried after 1, 5, 15, and 60 minutes, up to five attempts, before being recorded as permanently failed and reported as an exception. Acknowledging cancels anything still queued, and a cancelled delivery can never come back to life, though a send already in flight may still land once.

## Limits

| Thing | Limit |
|-------|-------|
| Layers per schedule | 20 |

| Restrictions per layer | 10 |

| Steps per escalation policy | 10 |

## Who can do what

| Action | Required role |
|--------|---------------|
| View teams, schedules, policies, and who is on call | Any member of the organization |
| Create or edit teams, schedules, and policies | Organization owner or admin |
| Create a schedule override | Any member of the organization |
| Delete a schedule override | Its creator, the person covering, or an organization owner or admin |
| Create an escalation channel and attach rules to it | Write access to the project |
| View, acknowledge, and resolve pages | Read access to the project. No write access needed. |
| Manage your own contact methods and notification rules | Yourself only |

## Configure on-call with the CLI or MCP

Everything above can also be done with the [write CLI](/learn/agent-write-access) `traceway-write`, or by an AI agent through the `traceway-write` skill or the write MCP server. Writes need **Allow agent writes** switched on for the organization, and the same roles as in the dashboard. [Agent Write Access](/learn/agent-write-access) covers the setup.

With an agent you describe the result:

```
/traceway-write Set up on-call for the Platform team: Maya and
Jonas rotate weekly, handoff Mondays at 09:00 Europe/Berlin.
Page whoever is on call first, and the whole team after 15
minutes without an acknowledgement.
```

By hand, each step is one command that takes a JSON file:

```bash
# Ids you will need: members (owners and admins only) and projects
traceway-write organization-members list --organization <org-id>
traceway-write projects list

# Steps 1 to 3 (organization owners and admins)
traceway-write teams create --organization <org-id> --file team.json
traceway-write schedules create --organization <org-id> --file schedule.json
traceway-write escalation-policies create --organization <org-id> --file policy.json

# Step 4: the escalation channel, in the selected project
traceway-write channels create --file channel.json

# Step 5: your own contact methods and notification chains
traceway-write contact-methods create --file contact.json
traceway-write notification-chains replace --file chains.json

# Check the result without paging anyone
traceway-write schedules timeline <schedule-id> --organization <org-id> \
  --from 2026-11-23T00:00:00Z --to 2026-12-07T00:00:00Z
traceway-write oncall now --organization <org-id>
traceway-write oncall current
```

`<org-id>` is the number of your organization. `traceway-write dashboards list` prints it as `organizationId`. Organization commands still need a selected project in that organization (`traceway-write projects use <project-id>`), which decides whose **Allow agent writes** switch applies.

The files, in the order of the steps. Replace the ids with the ones the previous commands returned:

```json filename="team.json"
{
  "name": "Platform",
  "description": "Checkout API responders",
  "memberUserIds": [1, 2],
  "projectIds": ["1e5b3c0f-0e8e-4652-b917-dfe3ce55ed11"]
}
```

```json filename="schedule.json"
{
  "teamId": 1,
  "name": "Platform primary",
  "description": "Weekly rotation, handoff Monday 09:00",
  "timezone": "Europe/Berlin",
  "definition": {
    "schemaVersion": 1,
    "layers": [
      {
        "name": "Primary",
        "rotationType": "weekly",
        "handoffDay": 1,
        "handoffTime": "09:00",
        "rotationStart": "2026-11-23",
        "userIds": [1, 2],
        "restrictions": []
      }
    ]
  }
}
```

`rotationType` is `daily`, `weekly` or `custom` (with `intervalDays`), and `handoffDay` runs from Monday = 1 to Sunday = 7. A restriction is `{"type":"daily","startTime":"18:00","endTime":"09:00"}`, or `"type":"weekly"` with `startDay` and `endDay` added.

```json filename="policy.json"
{
  "name": "Platform escalation",
  "definition": {
    "schemaVersion": 1,
    "urgency": "auto",
    "repeatCount": 1,
    "steps": [
      { "delayMinutes": 5, "targets": [{ "type": "schedule", "id": 1 }] },
      { "delayMinutes": 15, "targets": [{ "type": "team", "id": 1 }] }
    ]
  }
}
```

A target `type` is `schedule`, `team`, `user` or `channel`, and `urgency` is `auto`, `high` or `low`.

```json filename="channel.json"
{
  "name": "Platform pager",
  "channelType": "escalation",
  "config": { "policyId": 1 }
}
```

Route a rule to the channel with `traceway-write alerts create` or `alerts update`, described in [Alerts](/learn/alerts#configure-alerting-with-the-cli-or-mcp).

```json filename="contact.json"
{
  "methodType": "slack",
  "config": { "webhookUrl": "https://hooks.slack.com/services/T000/B000/XXXX" },
  "enabled": true
}
```

The other values of `methodType` are `email` (`config.email`, empty for your account email), `pushover` (`userKey`, `appToken`), `telegram` (`botToken`, `chatId`) and `sms` (`phoneNumber`). A new phone number is verified with `contact-methods verify <id> --file code.json`, where the file is `{"code":"123456"}`.

In a terminal every write asks `Continue? [y/N]`. An agent passes `--yes`.

A few behaviours to know before you change something that exists:

- **Updates replace the whole document.** Updating a schedule or a policy sends the complete definition, so read the current one first and keep the layers and steps you are not changing. `set-members` and `set-projects` replace the whole list (`{"userIds":[1,2]}`, `{"projectIds":["<project-id>"]}`).
- **`notification-chains replace` replaces both chains.** Keep the other urgency's chain in the file when you edit one. The methods in a chain must be your own, enabled and verified.
- **Personal settings are yours only.** Contact methods and notification chains can be changed only by their owner, also through an admin's credential.
- **Some commands send for real.** `contact-methods test` sends a real notification, adding or changing a phone number sends a verification code, and `channels test` on an escalation channel opens a real page.
- **Deleting a team deletes its schedules.**

## Server configuration

This section is for people who run their own Traceway instance. On Traceway Cloud the server is run for you and there is nothing to set here.

| Variable | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| `APP_BASE_URL` | The origin used to build the links in notifications, including acknowledge links. Set it. Pages are sent from a background worker with no request to take the origin from, so while this is unset every page links to `http://localhost:5173`. |
| `SMTP_ENABLED` | Must be `true` for email pages to be delivered. Anything else writes the email to the server log instead of sending it. |
| `TWILIO_ACCOUNT_SID` | Twilio account SID, required for SMS |
| `TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN` | Twilio auth token, required for SMS |
| `TWILIO_FROM_NUMBER` | Sending number. Use this or a messaging service. |
| `TWILIO_MESSAGING_SERVICE_SID` | Twilio messaging service, as an alternative to a from number |
| `ALLOW_PRIVATE_NOTIFICATION_TARGETS` | Set to `true` to let personal Slack contact methods point at private or loopback addresses. Off by default. |
| `ONCALL_POLL_SECONDS` | How often the escalator checks for pages that are due to escalate. Default 30, minimum 5. Separate from rule evaluation, so raising that interval never delays paging. |
| `OUTBOX_POLL_SECONDS` | How often queued notifications are sent. Default 15, minimum 5. |

### Email delivery

The account email is the fallback for every responder, and it only leaves the building if the server can send mail. Set `SMTP_ENABLED=true` along with the rest of the SMTP settings before you rely on on-call. With SMTP off, the page is written to the server log, which is fine for local development and a bad surprise in production.

### SMS and Twilio

SMS needs Twilio credentials on the server: `TWILIO_ACCOUNT_SID`, `TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN`, and one sender, either `TWILIO_FROM_NUMBER` or `TWILIO_MESSAGING_SERVICE_SID`.

Without them SMS is not offered at all. The type picker hides it, and the server rejects attempts to create one. This is deliberate: a channel that silently drops your 3am page is worse than no channel.

### Slack webhooks and private addresses

Slack is the one contact method type where the responder chooses the destination host. Every other type sends to a fixed service, so only the Slack webhook URL is checked: it must use http or https and resolve to a public address. A URL whose host does not resolve is rejected too, since it could never receive a page.

The check exists because contact methods are personal. They need no project role, so without it any member of the organization, read-only members included, could point a webhook at an internal address and use the test button to send requests into the server's own network.

If your paging destinations really live on a private network, for example a self-hosted Mattermost behind the same firewall as Traceway, set `ALLOW_PRIVATE_NOTIFICATION_TARGETS=true`. Project notification channels are not affected either way: the webhook channel type of [Alerts](/learn/alerts) exists to post anywhere, and creating one requires write access.

### Watching the queue

The `outbox` block of `/api/health/deep` reports the queue depth and the oldest pending delivery, so you can alert on your alerting. It is an operator endpoint behind the `HEALTH_DEEP_TOKEN` secret, and it is disabled while that is unset.

## When something goes wrong

| What you see | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A responder says they were never paged | Their contact method failed, was disabled, or was never added | Open the page and read its delivery log. Then have them add and test a [contact method](#step-5-add-your-contact-methods). |
| Pages only arrive by email | The responder has no contact methods, so the account email is used | [Step 5](#step-5-add-your-contact-methods) |
| No email arrives at all on a self-hosted instance | `SMTP_ENABLED` is not `true`, so emails go to the server log | [Email delivery](#email-delivery) |
| Links in notifications point to `localhost` | `APP_BASE_URL` is not set | [Server configuration](#server-configuration) |
| A page shows `exhausted` and stays open | The last step ran and **Repeat** is used up. Nothing more is sent. | Acknowledge or resolve it, and add a step or a repeat to the policy |
| A step notified nobody | No layer of the targeted schedule covers that moment | Close the gap on the schedule's timeline |
| **SMS (Twilio)** is missing from the **Type** list | The server has no Twilio credentials | [SMS and Twilio](#sms-and-twilio) |
| `This schedule is used by escalation policy(ies): ...` or `This team is used by escalation policy(ies): ...` | A policy that points at something deleted would page nobody, so the delete is refused | Remove those steps from the policy first |
| `This policy is used by notification channel(s): ...` | A channel still pages through the policy | Delete or re-point those channels first |
| `Verify this phone number before testing it.` | The SMS method is not verified yet | Enter the code, or request a new one |
| `Agent write access is turned off for this organization` | A CLI or MCP write while **Allow agent writes** is off | [Agent Write Access](/learn/agent-write-access#step-1-turn-on-agent-access) |
